## P1: Newborn SPL meta-analysis

**Blocking**

- **107 numbers on 40 lines are stale.** Fok 2005 was added to the data but not to the text. The new totals are 25 studies, 27 populations, n = 14,416 (previously 9,788) and 15 countries. The pooled mean is now 3.19 (3.04–3.34), with PI 2.41–3.98 and threshold 2.25 cm. Two findings change:
  - Egger p is now **0.088**, so the "no evidence of small-study effects" wording must change.
  - The WPR row in Table 2 changes completely, and the "k = 3 uninformative" note becomes obsolete.
- **Table 1 has no Fok row, and `Fok2005` is not in the verified reference tables,** so `build.py` will refuse the key once the row is added.
- **The PRISMA counts and Fig 1 are stale, and Fig 1 has no script that can rebuild it.** The full texts of Ishii 2014 and Gabrich 2007 are now in hand but are still logged as "not retrieved"; re-assess them.
- **Figures and text disagree:** Figs 2–4 were regenerated with k = 27, but the text still gives k = 26.

**Claims to correct:** "nearly the entire global PI" (it is 61%); "SD 0.7 almost twice anything observed" (it is about 1.2×); "validated against metafor" (no validation script exists).

**Data:** Boas 2006 (number measured at birth unknown), Feldman 1975 (whether ±0.7 is an SD), Romano-Riquer 2007 (the only population from the Americas), Bhakhri 2014 (India, n = 1,249).

## P2: Hormonal therapy meta-analysis

**Blocking**

- **"All but one study single-group" is wrong.** Chen 2015 is an RCT with untreated controls, Chalapathi used alternate allocation, Avril has two arms, and so do Liu 2021b and Agarwal.
- **17 of 39 arms vanish without a coded reason.** An arm-level flow and a characteristics table are needed.
- **The durability claim overreaches.** The Lambert median is 5.0 cm both at about 10 months and at 12.9 y, which means catch-down in SDS terms. Restate it in SDS.
- **Overclaims about regression to the mean.** The lower CI bounds for IM testosterone (0.19) and oral TU (−0.05) fall inside the RTM range, and the oral-TU CI includes 0.
- **The pre–post correlation r** is hard-coded from 2 of the 3 IPD studies (Tack, r = 0.96, dropped without explanation), and the sensitivity grid never goes below the base case.
- **Kohva 2019** (rFSH plus IM testosterone) is classed as IM testosterone. That classification drives the headline contrast, so a sensitivity analysis is needed.
- **Analyses promised but not reported:** the age and CHH meta-regressions and the SDS change.
- **Egger's test** on single-arm changes with I² = 96% is invalid.

**Data:** Chen 2015 (control-group change; check that the population is micropenis, not only hypospadias); Avril 2023 (open access; the package has Supplementary Table **1**, not 2). The duplicate-publication concern (Nerli 2013 / Kim 2011) still needs verifying, and Kim 2011 fails the 0–18 y rule.

## P3: Obesity and the apparent micropenis

**Blocking**

- **"Trivial deficit" is overstated.** The SMD −0.11 uses an SD pooled across ages 2–18, so it understates the within-age effect, and the difference is significant (p = 0.03). P4 shows that a 0.25 SD bias roughly doubles apparent prevalence.
- **The central conclusion** ("the difference largely disappears with the fat pad depressed") rests on one study that still found a difference. It must be softened, and the confounding of technique with design must be stated.
- **Referral series measure different things,** so "roughly a third" and "most micropenis in obese boys is buried penis" are unsupported.
- **Technique coding is stale** against full texts already held: Bhat 2015 = fat pad **not** depressed (this undercuts the thesis), Ishii 2014 = depressed, and Soydan 2012 is extractable.
- **Fig 2** omits Amirkashani and is not stratified as the text claims.
- **PRISMA:** "not retrieved = 0" is wrong; 32 of 39 reports were assessed on the abstract only.
- **Line 286** claims heterogeneous bias gives the largest inflation; P4's own data show otherwise.

**Data:** Mancini 2021 (non-stretched; values by BMI group); the Lee 2024 age-by-BMI tables; Ergashev 2021 (possible overlap with Lee); Tomova 2015; Hassan 2022.

## P4: Measurement-error simulation

All headline numbers reproduce byte-for-byte.

**Blocking**

- **Monte Carlo SE is not reported.** The PPV MCSE reaches 1.75 percentage points in the skewed scenarios, which contradicts "negligible".
- **λ conversions mix scales:**
  - Righi ICC gives λ ≈ 0.41, not 0.24, on the 0.40 cm anchor;
  - Goel's TEM is for boys under 4 years, not newborns.

  The abstract's "λ ≈ 0.25" is therefore unsupported.
- **Timing bias:** "about −1 SD and ten-fold" should be −0.55 to −0.85 SD and 4–8-fold.
- **Fat-pad bias of −0.5 SD has no source.** Relabel it as illustrative.
- **Placeholder** "[ANCHOR]" left at line 153.

**Data:** the Boas anchor (abstract only; the number measured at birth is unknown); Boas inter-observer ±0.67 cm (implies λ ≈ 0.6); a second reviewer for the 33 reliability rows.

## P5: SPL-REF protocol

**Blocking**

- **Enrolment 4,500 does not deliver the 4,279 analysable target;** about 4,800 is needed.
- **The minipuberty sub-cohort is mis-powered.** Boas r = 0.31 is for attained length; for growth r = 0.076, which needs about 1,356 infants.
- **Cole 2021**, the paper the sample size scales from, says 7,000–25,000 per sex. Justify the lower target by simulation or state it as a limitation.
- **The deconvolution formula is wrong:** between-examiner variance does not shrink with replicates.
- **Examiner and site numbers are missing,** and there is no inter-site calibration, so technique cannot be separated from population.
- **Tanner stage is partly defined by penile size,** so stage references are circular. Make testis volume the primary maturity covariate. This matches the app's new testis-volume reference.
- **Ethics approval:** "will be sought", with no committee named, and placeholder dates.

**SPIRIT gaps:** amendments, sponsor and committees, monitoring and adverse events, data management, consent forms, the laboratory and assays, sites and feasibility, the authorship policy, and the PPI statement written as a future plan.
